Insurance Cost Forecasting & Strategy Analysis

Case Study

Overview

As part of the company's annual employee health insurance renewal process, I developed a Monte Carlo simulation model to forecast future healthcare claims and evaluate alternative insurance strategies.

Rather than relying solely on historical averages, the model simulated thousands of possible claim scenarios, allowing management to understand financial risk under different outcomes and negotiate with providers using quantitative evidence.

Business Problem

Annual insurance renewal discussions relied heavily on insurer assumptions and historical claim summaries. Management required a better understanding of expected claim utilization, potential financial exposure, and whether alternative funding models could reduce long-term costs.

Methodology

  • Collected historical employee healthcare utilization data.
  • Modelled claim frequency using a Poisson distribution.
  • Executed 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations to estimate future claim scenarios.
  • Compared projected costs across multiple insurance renewal options.
  • Evaluated an Administrative Services Only (ASO) funding model as an alternative to conventional insurance.

Business Impact

  • Supported negotiations with prospective insurance providers using quantitative forecasting.
  • Contributed to significantly lower premium proposals despite historically high claim utilization.
  • Introduced an Administrative Services Only (ASO) alternative to improve financial transparency and cost control.
  • The ASO provider agreed to reduce the required security deposit after reviewing projected utilization scenarios.

Stakeholder Outcome

While the simulation demonstrated potential financial advantages, management ultimately did not proceed with the ASO option due to organizational risk preferences and previous experiences with similar arrangements.

The CFO independently reviewed the underlying data and simulation assumptions before considering the recommendations, highlighting the importance of transparency and reproducibility when using quantitative models for executive decision-making.

Lessons Learned

  • Quantitative models improve decision quality but do not replace executive judgement.
  • Successful analytics projects require stakeholder confidence as much as statistical accuracy.
  • Communicating assumptions clearly is just as important as producing accurate forecasts.
  • Business decisions are influenced by organizational experience, risk appetite, and financial priorities in addition to analytical evidence.
