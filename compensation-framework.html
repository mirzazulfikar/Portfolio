Localized Job Evaluation & Compensation Architecture

Designing a structured, data-driven compensation framework to connect job complexity, organizational growth, and local market conditions.

Prototype Validation Pending

Project Overview

As part of my annual Compensation & Benefits objectives, I designed a proposed job evaluation and compensation architecture for a rapidly growing organization.

The framework was created to address increasing differences in job scope, responsibilities, and organizational complexity while providing a consistent mechanism for translating job value into compensation structures across different geographic markets.

Rather than relying primarily on job titles or individual salary negotiations, the proposed framework evaluates the structural requirements of each role and connects the resulting job grade to localized market compensation data.

The Business Problem

Rapid organizational growth can cause job responsibilities to evolve faster than formal job descriptions and salary structures.

This creates several potential risks:

  • Job titles may no longer accurately represent the complexity of the underlying role.
  • Employees performing significantly different levels of work may occupy similar titles.
  • Salary decisions can become increasingly dependent on individual negotiation and historical precedent.
  • Expansion into new geographic markets can make a single compensation structure difficult to apply consistently.

The objective was therefore to create a framework that separates job value from job title and separates structural job architecture from local market economics.

The Architecture

The proposed framework consists of three primary layers.

01 · Job Evaluation

Evaluate the structural requirements of a job using a Hay-style framework covering:

  • Know-How
  • Problem Solving
  • Accountability

02 · Job Architecture

Translate the evaluated job complexity into a structured grade architecture using a geometric progression.

The framework uses the proposed 15% complexity progression as its architectural constraint.

03 · Local Compensation

Connect the resulting job architecture to local compensation conditions using geographic market anchors.

The resulting Rpay parameter is therefore expected to differ between markets.

Job Evaluation Methodology

The framework evaluates the job itself, rather than the individual occupying the position.

Management-provided job descriptions establish the formal expectations of the role. Employee interviews are then used to identify differences between the documented role and the responsibilities actually performed.

Where significant differences are identified, the job profile can be recalibrated before the position is evaluated.

→
Employee Interview Actual responsibilities
→
Gap Analysis Identify scope differences
→
Job Recalibration Update structural requirements
→
Evaluation Determine job grade

This approach is intended to reduce the risk of title inflation and prevent job descriptions from being the sole source of truth when organizational responsibilities have evolved.

The 15% Geometric Step Rule

The proposed architecture follows the geometric progression underlying the Hay Job Evaluation methodology.

Rather than treating every grade as an equal linear increment, each progression represents an approximately 15% increase in structural complexity.

The 15% figure is therefore treated as an architectural constraint of the evaluation framework, rather than a parameter empirically estimated from the organization's salary data.

Committee Calibration Test

When evaluating progression between two levels, the committee asks whether the role demonstrates a meaningful increase in structural requirements sufficient to justify movement to the next level.

From a quantitative perspective, the 15% progression should therefore be understood as an a priori assumption of the architecture rather than an empirically validated constant.

Localized Compensation Modeling

A key design principle of the framework is that job architecture and compensation economics should not necessarily use the same parameters.

The job evaluation structure can remain consistent across countries while the compensation progression is recalculated using local market conditions.

Market Anchoring

The compensation curve uses geographic market anchors rather than assuming that a universal salary progression rate can be applied across all locations.

Floor

The regional legal minimum wage provides the lower compensation anchor.

Target Market

Head-hunter benchmarks and publicly available market sources are used to establish the upper market anchor.

The resulting progression parameter is then calculated based on the relationship between these anchors and the number of structural grades in the architecture.

Governance & Recalibration

The framework is designed as a structured decision-support system rather than an automated mechanism for determining individual employee pay.

  • Formal market recalibration is planned on a two-year cycle.
  • Market conditions can be monitored between formal recalibration cycles.
  • Significant changes in job scope can trigger re-evaluation of the position.
  • Job evaluation should be based on structural requirements rather than incumbent performance.
  • Compensation exceptions such as scarce skills, retention requirements, or employees outside modeled ranges require additional policy decisions.

Known Limitations

This framework is currently a proposed architecture and has not yet been implemented or validated across the organization's full job population.

Not Yet Validated

The relationship between evaluated job complexity and the resulting grade structure requires testing against real positions.

Market Sensitivity

The resulting Rpay parameter depends on the quality and comparability of the market anchors.

Scarce Skills

The framework does not yet define a finalized mechanism for handling market premiums associated with scarce or critical skills.

Incumbent Positioning

A formal policy for employees whose existing compensation falls above or below the modeled salary range remains to be developed.

Proposed Validation Roadmap

Before full implementation, the framework should be tested against a representative sample of jobs.

  1. Select representative positions across different functions and organizational levels.
  2. Conduct independent job evaluations and calibration.
  3. Compare modeled grades against current organizational structures.
  4. Benchmark resulting salary ranges against external market data.
  5. Conduct sensitivity analysis using alternative market assumptions.
  6. Evaluate the resulting payroll impact.
  7. Identify exceptions and develop policies for them.
  8. Recalibrate the framework before broader implementation.

What I Learned

Designing the framework highlighted an important distinction between building a quantitative model and implementing an organizational system.

A mathematically coherent model does not automatically produce a successful compensation strategy. The model must also be supported by reliable market data, clear governance, stakeholder confidence, and policies for situations that fall outside the standard architecture.

The next stage of this project is therefore not simply to improve the mathematics, but to validate the assumptions against real organizational data and determine whether the resulting architecture produces sensible outcomes.
